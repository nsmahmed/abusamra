Goal seeking runs the learned port model in reverse. It tries every combination of X-ray lanes, inspection docks and their split by category, parking, pre-arrival filing, a green channel and shift overlap within the planner's limits. Each one is costed over five years from public unit costs. It then keeps three different answers.

How these numbers are made. The model learns from the 2026 gate-pass and declaration extracts. Duplicates are removed, rows are rolled up to one row per truck, stays over 7 days are dropped, and every figure is a median, as in the bottleneck report. A Gaussian-mixture generator draws synthetic trucks stage by stage, by goods category and X-ray. A queueing model calibrated to today's waits turns lanes, docks and bays into hours. Demand to 2030 follows the learned growth of food declarations, halved each year. Unit costs are public benchmarks converted to QAR (see config.json and docs/AI.md). The figures are synthetic, built to behave like the port, not to report it.
